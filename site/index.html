Geometry first. Names only when earned.

Most of a capture is room, and a wall that stays in the cloud welds everything standing against it into one blob. So extraction starts by taking the room away.

  • Each gaussian knows its surface. The thinnest axis of its ellipsoid is a normal. Removing walls whole cut the largest component from 24 to 3.8 percent of the capture.
  • A name has to pass a test. A valve needs pipework within half a metre, and a pump needs something under it. Fail, and the label stays object with the reason attached.
  • Vision models stay offline. SAM 3 masks from posed views are lifted into 3D through the splat's own depth and fused. A person reviews the result, and it ships as a fixture, so nothing runs at demo time.

Runs in your browser on a made-up floor, with the cost rule from nav.py. Tap the grid to add an obstacle.

A* that keeps to the middle of the aisle.

Shortest is not the same as safest. Every step costs more as it nears an obstacle, so the route gives machines room without being told where the aisles are.

  • The grid comes from the scan. 10 cm cells, with obstacles inflated by the robot's radius and a 2 cm margin. Ground nobody observed is not walkable.
  • Corners stay clear. The search is 8-connected, and a diagonal step between two obstacles is refused.
  • It replans live. Drop an obstacle in the viewer and the route is recomputed from where the robot stands. When nothing fits, the answer is reachable: false with a reason.

The robot goes behind the machine, not over it.

Splats are blended back to front and write no depth, so anything added to the scene would float on top of the room. PlantPilot draws the capture twice to fix that.

  • Depth first. A colourless pass writes the capture into the depth buffer. It draws every second gaussian, which leaves no gaps at half the fill.
  • Only solid surfaces count. A high opacity cut keeps faint gaussians out, because they would punch invisible holes in whatever is behind them.
  • No depth model needed. The splat's own depth beat TIPSv2 with a DPT head: 52 against 0 percent of silhouette pixels within one pixel, and a boundary F1 of 0.137 against 0.021.

The viewer's draw order running live on a generated scene, not the capture.

The room is an input, not a constant.

Up axis, floor height and voxel size are measured from each capture. The robot is a parameter too, so the same rules give each body its own route, clearance and reachable floor.

What is real, what is proposed and what is synthetic.

The viewer keeps these apart on screen too, so a pack condition never reads as a measurement of the machine it was assigned to.

Real

The capture
A supplied scan. Units are treated as metres, and no door-height scale check was done.
3D frames and route
Computed from the capture. 122 smaller clusters fall outside the 150-object cap, which is why the route also uses the floor, wall and overhead layers.

Proposed

Equipment mapping
A match score, not a calibrated probability. The pack has no measured positions, so P-005 on OBJ-037 is a proposal, not an identification.
Class labels
Geometric proposals. 8 objects are named by multi-view fusion, and SAM 3 text prompts were tried and not used for placing assets.

Synthetic

Telemetry and alarms
Supplied synthetic Valmet data, labelled as a scenario in the viewer.
The robot
A viewer marker following the planned path at 0.5 m/s, not a physics simulation.
