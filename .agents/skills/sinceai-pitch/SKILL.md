# The pitch is a demo with a script

Two audiences, two lengths, one product.

- The voter: 20 seconds of attention, 20 comparisons, and only the submission text and a two minute video. This vote decides the five finalists.
- The finals jury: 4 minutes on stage and 6 minutes of questions, at EduCity on Sunday from 14:00.

## The 20 second test

Write the submission answer first and test it on someone who has not seen the project. They should be able to say back who has the problem, what the product does, and what it shows. If they cannot, the text is too long or too abstract. Aim for one sentence for the problem, one for the product, and one for the proof.

## The 4 minute pitch

| Beat | Time | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0:00 to 0:20 | One person, one number, one sentence. No company history. |
| Problem | 0:20 to 0:50 | Who pays for this today, and what it costs them. |
| Product | 0:50 to 2:20 | The live demo, one path, no feature tour. Narrate what the viewer sees, not the architecture. |
| Proof | 2:20 to 3:00 | The number, the before/after, or the moment that shows it works. Say the number once. |
| Trust | 3:00 to 3:30 | Where the data goes, what is mocked, what is real, who owns the output. |
| Ask | 3:30 to 4:00 | What the prize or the next 90 days unlocks. One sentence. |

Show the product for at least two of the four minutes. No slide that is only a diagram. Rehearse to 3:45 rather than 4:00, because adrenaline adds time.

## The 6 minute Q&A

Prepare answers, not slides. The jury asks about what the judge guide scores.

- Does it work: have a fallback, the recorded clip, the seeded data, or a second path.
- Business value: one number, one customer, one price. Never "huge market".
- Technical depth: the one hard part and how you solved it, with the architecture known by heart.
- Data and privacy: the data lineage in three sentences, including what is mocked.
- Feasibility: what breaks first at a hundred times the load, and what you would fix.
- Team: who did what, and what you learned that you did not know on Friday.
- The trap: "what would you cut?" Have an answer; it shows judgement.

Never answer an unknown question with a guess. Say what you know, what you would test, and how long it would take.

## The demo video

Two minutes, recorded before the deadline and linked on the submission form. Use the shot list in `SUBMISSION.md`: title card, the problem in one scene, the core job end to end with no cuts, the proof, responsible handling, close. Record it cleanly once and add captions. A phone recording of a working screen beats a perfect edit that ships at 09:59.

## Rehearsal

- Run the pitch end to end three times: alone, to the team, and to someone outside the project.
- Time every run and cut whichever beat runs long.
- Practise the failure: what you say when the network or the model call dies during the demo.
- Put the numbers and names on one card. Do not read from the deck during Q&A.
